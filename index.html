aasadi [at] ncsu [dot] edu

I am a graduate student in Mechanical Engineering, with a minor in Electrical and Computer Engineering, at North Carolina State University, supervised by Prof. Nitin Sharma in the Neuromuscular Control and Robotics Laboratory. My research focuses on learning-enabled control of nonlinear robotic systems with formal safety guarantees. I combine reinforcement learning and model predictive control with control barrier functions and reachability analysis, with applications to legged robots and human-interactive robots such as lower-limb exoskeletons.

Before coming to NC State, I received my B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Iran University of Science and Technology, where I worked in the Intelligent Robotics Laboratory at the intersection of control theory and reinforcement learning, building dynamic models and learning-enabled controllers for wheeled-legged and flexible manipulators.

projects

NC State · 2024–present

Lower-limb exoskeleton balance-recovery platform

An exoskeleton framework for perturbation recovery that brings together rigid-body dynamics, contact constraints, actuator limits and center-of-mass regulation. It pairs robotic actuation with functional electrical stimulation, and I evaluate safety-critical controllers on it in simulation and in experimental balance-recovery trials.

IUST

Learning-based control of a flexible revolute–prismatic manipulator

Dynamic models and MATLAB simulations for end-effector regulation and vibration suppression, comparing PID, fuzzy-PID, Q-learning and multi-agent RL, including RL-based tuning of PID gains and fuzzy rules.

IUST

Reinforcement learning for robot control

SAC and DDPG agents trained in simulation for ball balancing with a 7-DoF manipulator, quadruped locomotion, and adaptive cruise control.

IUST · RoboCup

Autonomous mobile robot

As team leader and programmer, I led the design and build of an autonomous mobile robot for the RoboCup Autonomous Cars League.
